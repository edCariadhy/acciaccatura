# NNNN — short title in the imperative

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD

## Context

What was true that forced a choice. Numbers where there are numbers — a
measurement is worth more to the next reader than an adjective. Say what the
constraint was, not just what the feature was.

## Options

Every option that was seriously on the table, including the one taken. For each,
what it buys and what it costs. Be fair to the ones that lost: a strawman here
is worse than no page, because it invites the reader to reopen the question with
the same bad summary.

### The one that looks obvious

If there is an approach a newcomer would reach for first, say so explicitly and
say what happens if they do. This is the highest-value paragraph on most of
these pages.

## Decision

What was chosen, in one or two sentences. Then the reason it beat the others —
which is usually about a constraint, not a preference.

## Consequences

What this costs, what it now makes hard, and what has to be kept true for it to
keep working. Include the parts that are annoying; a record that only lists
upsides is not being honest and will not be believed.

## What would change this

The evidence that would justify revisiting. If the answer is "nothing", say that
and say why. Without this a record reads as permanent, and a reader with new
information has no way in.
